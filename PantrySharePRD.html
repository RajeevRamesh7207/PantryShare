PantryShare

Food Bank Distribution Planning

Help a regional food bank's distribution coordinator allocate donated and purchased food to partner pantries fairly and before it expires.

Industry
Nonprofit
Primary user
Distribution coordinator
Format
Two-week MVP
Data
Synthetic only

Overview

Scenario

A regional food bank supplies 60 partner pantries, shelters, and school programs. Stock arrives from grocery donations, farms, and bulk purchases, with very different shelf lives. Partners differ in size, in storage (some have no freezer), and in the communities they serve.

Each week the coordinator decides how much of each item goes to each partner, balancing fair shares by need against what partners can store and collect. Poor allocation means food spoils in the warehouse while some pantries run short.

Primary userA distribution coordinator responsible for weekly allocations to partner agencies.

Example situation

A grocery chain donates 2 tonnes of yogurt that expires in 6 days, and a farm drops off 3 tonnes of potatoes. Twelve partners have refrigeration, but four of them have already collected this week. A rural pantry serving a growing number of families has asked for more protein, and frozen chicken is running low. The coordinator must decide how to allocate.

Data

Information available to the team

Assume the following synthetic inventory and partner data are available, covering three months of distribution:

  • Warehouse inventory with item, category, quantity, and expiry date
  • Partner list with households served, storage capacity, and pickup schedule
  • Past allocations and pickup records
  • Partner requests and notes
  • Nutrition category targets
  • Delivery route capacity

Known data issues

The dataset contains out-of-date figures for households served, donated items with unclear expiry dates, partners that do not collect what was allocated, and inventory counts recorded in different units.

Challenge

Challenge

Create a two-week MVP that helps the coordinator build a fair, explainable weekly allocation that keeps waste low.

These features define the expected depth of the MVP:

  1. Allocation suggestionsSuggest quantities of each item for each partner based on need, storage, and pickup schedule.
  2. Expiry and fairness checksFlag stock likely to expire before distribution and partners receiving well above or below their fair share.
  3. ExplanationsShow why each partner received what it did.

Team decisions

The brief deliberately leaves these open. There's no single correct answer; the team makes and justifies its own call on each one, and that choice will shape the solution.

  1. How should need be measured?
  2. How should short-dated stock be prioritized?
  3. How should partners that miss pickups be treated?
  4. How should nutritional balance be weighed against quantity?

Expected MVP outcome

The coordinator should be able to load inventory and partner data, see an allocation plan with expiry and fairness flags, and adjust an allocation and see totals and storage checks update.

Out of scope

The MVP does not need to track individual clients, manage volunteers, handle fundraising, plan delivery routes, or connect to the food bank's inventory system.

Validate

UAT scenarios

The solution should be testable against cases such as:

  1. A normal week with enough stock
  2. A large donation of short-dated dairy
  3. A partner without refrigeration
  4. A partner that missed last week's pickup
  5. A rural partner with growing need
  6. An item counted in different units
  7. Total requests that exceed available stock

Success measures

Teams set targets and show evidence for measures such as:

  • Reduction in spoiled food
  • Fairness of distribution relative to need
  • Time spent building the weekly plan
  • Share of allocations collected
  • Coordinator and partner confidence in the plan

Key constraints

  1. The MVP must work with synthetic inventory and partner data only.
  2. Perishable items must not be allocated to partners without suitable storage.
  3. No personal data about individual clients may be used.
  4. The distribution coordinator retains final control over every allocation.
